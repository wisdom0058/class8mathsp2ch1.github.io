Question

A bar graph shows the percentage of people who can use computers in each age group (teens, twenties, thirties, … 60+), for men and women. Which statements are valid?
(i) People in their twenties are the most computer-literate. (ii) Women lag behind men across age groups. (iii) There are more people in their twenties than teenagers. (iv) More than a quarter of people in their thirties can use computers. (v) Less than 1 in 10 aged 60+ can use computers. (vi) Half of the people in their twenties can use computers.
🔊 Audio explanation (step by step)

Solution

The graph shows percentages within each age group, not the number of people.
(i) The tallest bars are for the twenties group → True.
(ii) In every age group the women's bar is lower than the men's bar → True.
(iii) A percentage graph cannot tell us how many people are in each group → Not valid (cannot be concluded).
(iv) The thirties bars are below 25% → False.
(v) The 60+ bars are below 10% → True.
(vi) The twenties bars are below 50% → False.

The solutions PDF marks (iii) as True, but a percentage graph gives no information about group sizes, so (iii) cannot be concluded from the graph.

Valid statements: (i), (ii) and (v).
