1.4 Growth and Compounding

← Q35☰ All QuestionsQ37 →

Question

Consider ₹1000 growing at 10% p.a. How long will it take to double with compounding vs. without compounding? Is compounding an example of exponential growth and not-compounding an example of linear growth?
🔊 Audio explanation (step by step)

Solution

Without compounding

Interest per year = 10% of 1000 = ₹100 (fixed)
To double we need ₹1000 of interest: 1000 ÷ 100 = 10 years

With compounding

Each year the amount is multiplied by 1.1. We need (1.1)ᵗ = 2.

After 7 years ₹1949 (< 2000), after 8 years ₹2144 (> 2000). So it doubles between 7 and 8 years, about 7.3 years.

Type of growth

Without compounding the amount goes up by the same ₹100 every year – a straight line → linear growth.
With compounding the amount is multiplied by the same 1.1 every year – it curves upward faster and faster → exponential growth.

Doubling takes 10 years without compounding and about 7.3 years with compounding. Yes – compounding is exponential growth; no compounding is linear growth.
